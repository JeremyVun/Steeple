## Assessment

The strongest opportunity is to make every booking understandable and recoverable. A host needs to know who is responsible, what will happen, how payment is arranged, and what help exists if something goes wrong. A guest needs enough concrete information to make that commitment without an exploratory booking. The current system contains useful booking mechanics, but several of those decisions and assurances are incomplete or arrive too late.

Keep anonymous browsing, sign-in at commitment, structured activity/group/schedule information, messaging after confirmation, host approval and counter-offers, per-session payment state, and the double-blind review mechanism. Do not add a long universal verification form or make new users look untrustworthy because they have no ratings.

Astra's visual review recommends retaining the warm paper surfaces, existing typography, sage/terracotta palette, map/list pairing, restrained room cards, and four-step host listing flow. The consequential visual changes concern scheduling controls, decision information, and mobile action placement. A focused booking-form comp is justified; a broad visual redesign is not.

This is an expert review, not a user study. Public production reads were verified on 1 October 2026. ## Priority findings

### 1. Correct the live trust and payment claims before inviting unfamiliar users

**Evidence: live and source.** [Live terms](https://steeple.jeremyvun.com/terms.html) still show version 2026-08-07 and describe Steeple collecting payments and confirming identity through Google/Apple. [Live flags](https://steeple.jeremyvun.com/api/v1/flags?platform=web) return `payments.enabled:false`. Current source explicitly says real guest payments are not implemented; Stripe host onboarding is sandbox-only. Source terms dated 2026-09-20 correctly describe direct payment and distinguish sign-in from identity verification.

The live sitemap/catalog also exposes only **Haunted House / haunted room** and **Hogwarts / dining room**, priced at $1 and $5 per hour. Their public records have empty house rules, parking/transit details, and contact email. This is understandable preview inventory, but it cannot demonstrate a trustworthy marketplace to strangers.

**Recommendation:** treat the existing release-hardening work and real pilot inventory as the first priority. Put accurate payment and verification statements at the booking decision, not only in terms. Clearly identify a demonstration if sample inventory remains publicly accessible. Use a few complete, real pilot listings before broader acquisition. This review did not deploy, unpublish, or alter production.

**Existing work:** [mvp-release.md](runbooks/mvp-release.md) already records the undeployed corrections, real-supply gate, and need for a monitored support contact. This is release completion, not a new redesign project. See [payments contract](contracts/payments.md), [source terms](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/public/terms.html), and [payment copy](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/money.js).

### 2. Make the commitment explicit before either side accepts

**Evidence: source.** The room CTA always says “Request this space”; the composer can then say “Book this space” for instant booking. Its heading remains “Booking request”. The composer shows an hourly rate and schedule/count, but no calculated session/series total, collection method, or cancellation summary. Payment instructions are available in the confirmed booking. A signed-in guest can submit directly from the composer.

**Recommendation:** provide one review of the actual commitment, incorporating the existing fields rather than adding another questionnaire. Show selected dates and venue timezone, event and setup/cleanup hours, number attending, amount per session, total across a bounded series, collection method, applicable cancellation terms, and whether this action confirms a booking or requests approval. A direct-payment booking must say that the host collects payment and identify how and when it is due once agreed. Do not imply a platform refund for money the platform never collected.

Use consistent actions: “Check dates” while exploring, then “Request booking” or “Confirm booking” when the outcome is known. Keep material rules beside that commitment and snapshot the accepted rules, pricing, and cancellation terms with the booking. Price snapshots already exist; room rules remain mutable listing text. This saved agreement is also the basis for resolving later disagreements.

The quoted price also needs to survive the approval period. Current confirmation computes the price from the room's rate at confirmation time; the application does not store the guest's quoted price. Preserve the quote at request time and require renewed acceptance of a changed amount or counter-offer total. Otherwise an approval-based booking can confirm a different price from the one the guest saw.

Carry the same price, collection method, next action, and booking link into confirmation messages. Current source confirmation emails explain the schedule and booking status, but offline confirmations omit payment instructions; a person who relies on email should receive the same essential facts as someone who reopens the app. Keep the existing restraint on recurring reminder emails.

See [roomPanel.js](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/roomPanel.js), [composer.js](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/guest/composer.js), [Booking.cs](../src/Steeple.Persistence/Models/Booking.cs), and [Room.cs](../src/Steeple.Persistence/Models/Room.cs).

### 3. Give hosts an explicit booking-control choice before publication

**Evidence: source; both live listings currently report instant mode.** New venues default to `instant`. The listing wizard does not send a booking-mode choice; the host can change it later on the desk. Publication copy says groups can “send you a request”, which can create the expectation of approval even when the next guest books immediately.

**Recommendation:** make “I approve each request” versus instant booking a required, clearly explained choice in listing setup, with the consequence repeated in the publish summary. For the initial stranger-to-host pilot, recommend approval for first-time guests while allowing hosts to opt into instant booking. This changes the current product default and needs Jeremy's decision; it is not an existing requirement being rediscovered.

Later, introduce an optional “Allow guests I have hosted before to book instantly” rule. Explain eligibility and exclusions rather than hiding them behind a trust score. A guest who does not qualify should normally be routed to a request, preserving a route for new groups.

See [manage contract](contracts/manage.md), [saveVenue](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/manage.js), [listing flow](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/listing.js), and [desk mode control](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/desk.js).

### 4. Build a small, complete issue-resolution flow

**Evidence: source.** There is correspondence, cancellation, no-show marking, and ratings, but no structured incident/report API or user flow for damage, access failure, cleanliness, payment disagreement, or resolution tracking. The terms say to let Steeple know about problems but provide no contact route. A payment-status token named `disputed` is not an implemented venue dispute process.

**Recommendation:** put “Report a problem” on every booking. Start with the affected session, issue type, short account, optional evidence, and desired outcome. Pre-fill the booking, parties, agreed terms, messages, schedule, and relevant payment records. Show who must respond and what happens next. Give the other party a chance to reply; keep disputed allegations separate from resolved findings. A resolution records the outcome and any agreed action. Financial execution should distinguish “refund requested”, “refund issued”, and direct-payment arrangements.

For a small pilot, one operator queue with clear ownership and a realistic response expectation is enough. A monitored support contact is the immediate first step; a decorative reporting button without someone receiving the case would make the experience worse. Do not promise insurance, damage reimbursement, emergency response, or automatic deductions. Those require separate operating policies and payment capabilities.

Add a short care checklist generated from the host's confirmed rules: arrival/access, permitted areas, setup, cleaning, and closing up. Let hosts adjust it. Optional issue photos and a simple “Everything was left as agreed” response produce useful records with much less effort than requiring every guest to document every visit. Preserve evidence for an open case rather than allowing ordinary correspondence cleanup to remove it.

See [applications/bookings contract](contracts/applications.md), [payments contract](contracts/payments.md), and [booking controller](../src/Steeple.Api/Controllers/Bookings/BookingsController.cs).

### 5. Make reputation useful and contestable before using it to filter guests

**Evidence: source.** Host request details show signed-in status, revealed ratings, and annual no-show counts. `hasPaymentMethod` and `completedBookings` are available in the data path but not presented in the host's trust summary. Reputation summaries are absent until a rating is revealed, so an unrated person's completed-booking history is unavailable there. No-show marking is unilateral; after the session starts it can become final, with no exposed response or correction route. The UI explicitly says the report is final and becomes part of trust history.

**Recommendation:** generate a compact factual summary from records: completed bookings, previous bookings with this venue, relevant revealed feedback, and actual payment state when supported. Explain what each signal establishes. A card on file is not payment received; payment received is not a payout delivered to the host. Retain the useful absence of empty stars for newcomers. Offer a clear first-booking route instead of applying a low score.

Change a no-show action into a report: notify the other party, allow a response or correction, and distinguish an unresolved report from an upheld outcome. Do not automatically blacklist or downgrade someone based on one allegation. A completed calendar period is also not proof of good conduct; label counts accurately.

Use reusable group/event details to reduce repeated writing, but let guests edit them for each booking. Prompt only for information relevant to the host's published conditions. Organisation affiliation can be optional, labelled as self-reported unless checked, and should not exclude informal groups.

Make responsibility concrete: confirm whether the organiser will be the person on site and collect an alternative responsible contact only when needed. Reuse that answer in the host's request summary and arrival/closing instructions. The host should not have to infer who will collect keys, supervise the group, and leave the room as agreed from a persuasive paragraph.

If event descriptions are drafted with AI, keep the underlying facts reviewable and confirmed by the organiser. Fluent writing must not become an eligibility signal; the app should make a genuine first request easier to express without pretending to establish a person's reliability.

See [host trust presentation](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/letter.js), [host model](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/model.js), [RatingService.cs](../src/Steeple.Api/Services/Ratings/RatingService.cs), and [MarkNoShowAsync](../src/Steeple.Api/Services/Bookings/BookingService.cs).

### 6. Give guests the practical information and questions needed before booking

**Evidence: live and source.** The API supports parking, transit, and public contact details, and the venue panel can render parking/transit when present. The web listing form currently collects name, address, and description without corresponding practical-information inputs. Its save payload omits those fields. Rooms have amenities/accessibility labels and rules, but neither current live listing supplies a useful description or rules. There is no clear pre-booking host-question action in the room/venue panels; the thread belongs to an application.

**Recommendation:** help hosts complete a short, reusable venue profile: getting there, arrival/entry, accessibility specifics, toilets, furniture/equipment, permitted activities, noise/finish times, and setup/cleanup expectations. Prompt for a useful room photo plus entrance/layout/facility views when relevant. Distinguish “not provided” from “not available”; never infer an accessible route from an accessible parking checkbox.

Put “Ask the host” and optionally “Arrange a viewing” before commitment. A guest should not have to create an instant booking to find out whether the space is suitable. Keep exact door codes and other sensitive access instructions restricted to appropriate confirmed guests.

Collect these facts once at the venue/room level and show them where needed; do not turn every booking into a long checklist. Warn the host about meaningful gaps using direct links to the missing fields.

See [place panel](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/listing/place.js), [describe panel](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/listing/describe.js), [manage payload](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/host/manage.js), and [venue panel](../src/Steeple.Web.v2/src/ui/venuePanel.js).

## Low-friction trust model to aim for

1. **Browse without an account.** Show factual venue information and costs before asking anything of the guest.
2. **Describe the event once.** Reuse group name, contact person, activity, numbers, and ordinary requirements; reconfirm changes for each booking.
3. **Check fit automatically.** Match dates, capacity, accepted activity, and explicit host conditions. Explain conflicts and offer a request path for discretionary exceptions.
4. **Show evidence to the host.** Generate a summary from completed bookings, prior visits to this venue, revealed reviews, and payment facts. Clearly label self-reported information.
5. **Ask for extra evidence only when necessary.** Use a host's published, relevant requirements; avoid default ID uploads, compulsory organisation membership, or a long first-booking interview.
6. **Record the shared agreement.** Save the dates, price, rules, responsibilities, and cancellation terms both sides accepted. Generate the visit checklist and reminders from that record.
7. **Handle exceptions with a person.** Automation can assemble a timeline, request missing evidence, and route a case. Disputed fault, penalties, and reputation sanctions need an accountable decision and a correction path.

Do not build a single hidden “trust score” as the first version. Explicit eligibility rules and factual histories are easier for hosts to understand and easier for genuine newcomers to satisfy. Start with deterministic summaries; AI is optional for drafting and organisation, with the original evidence available and generated text reviewable.

The community purpose can become tangible without adding another gate. Let a host briefly explain what venue hire supports, and give them a private summary of actual booked hours and returning groups. Treat these as modest follow-ups after the booking basics; avoid invented impact claims or a public popularity competition.

## Suggested sequence and success checks

For eventual platform payments, give hosts a booking-level money record: guest payment status, gross amount, disclosed fees, net amount, payout status/expected arrival, and the action needed if blocked. Avoid one “Paid” label doing the work of both collection and payout. Peerspace's [published payout states](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119437-what-do-my-payout-statuses-mean) make this distinction explicit. Its [issue process](https://support.peerspace.com/en/articles/10119177-what-do-i-do-if-there-is-an-issue-resulting-from-a-booking) also ties reports to booking evidence and informs both parties. These are useful workflow references, checked 1 October 2026; their deadlines, fees, protections, and operating commitments are not proposed Steeple policies.

| Stage | Deliverable | How to judge it |

| Before a stranger-based pilot | Accurate deployed claims, real complete pilot listings, visible monitored help, deliberate host booking mode. | Each participant can explain who takes payment, whether approval is needed, and who handles a problem. |
| First product improvement | Commitment summary, reusable practical venue information, pre-booking questions, saved booking terms. | A guest can assess fit and cost without sending a booking to ask a question; a host can approve without chasing routine missing details. |

### Observed interaction problems

- **Live mobile scheduling:** at 390×844, the calendar header began around y=1050, below the initial view. Half-hour cells measured 33.28×10 CSS pixels and week arrows 26×26. The scrollable sheet contained 1456 pixels of content in a 726-pixel viewport. Put date, start, and end inputs before the long event description; retain the calendar as a useful availability view.
- **Other live mobile controls:** several header actions, filter chips, map controls, and back controls were around 27–32 pixels high. Enlarge the interactive area to the product's 44×44 target without making every visible icon oversized.
- **Host decisions in authenticated fixtures:** at phone width, approval controls began around y=1200 after prose and messages; eight schedule labels collided. Bring a compact schedule, event, payment, and responsibility summary near the heading, keep decisions easy to find, and reduce chart labels while retaining exact times in text. These are locally rendered deployed-UI observations, not a signed-in production test.
- **Host entry:** the live desk leads with generic sign-in. Explain control over bookings, the actual payment arrangement, and the available help before asking a prospective host to proceed. Only Apple sign-in was observed; this review does not treat an additional provider as a release requirement.

Provide an equivalent way to set the entire start/end range using taps or ordinary inputs. Keyboard support alone does not establish a non-dragging pointer alternative. W3C explains these separately in [Dragging Movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements) and [Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum). The latter has a 24×24 CSS-pixel minimum with defined exceptions; the project's 44×44 target remains the design goal. This review is not a full WCAG conformance assessment.

### Three alternatives, one recommendation

| Direction | Change | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **A: One page, dates first — selected** | Explicit date/time inputs first, existing event fields retained, cost and commitment summary before submission. | Smallest useful change, with medium implementation scope. The completed recurring form remains long on a phone. |
| **B: Dates → plans → review** | Split the same information into three steps with a final review. | More guidance, but more navigation for returning guests and more draft/history handling. Larger scope. |
| **C: Editable request summary** | Assemble schedule, group, plans, and rules through inline edits; the completed request becomes its own review. | Compact completed view, but first-time editing is less obvious and focus/error handling is more complex. Larger scope. |

Use **A as the starting point**. Borrowing **C's compact completed-request summary** for the final check remains a recommendation, not a separate accepted ruling. Do not combine all three navigation systems. B becomes worthwhile if observed first-time guests struggle with the long single page; C warrants further investment if editing and repeat bookings dominate actual use.
